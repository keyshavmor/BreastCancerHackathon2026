# The four design strategies

Candidates were generated from four competing hypotheses about *where* to edit the
antibody, run in parallel so the portfolio tests several ideas rather than fifteen
variations of one. The internal code appears in the data files; the label is what
you should say out loud.

| Code | Strategy | Where the edits go | How it performed |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `A_h3` | **CDR-H3 focused** | edits concentrated in CDR-H3, the antibody's main binding loop | scored WORST here, contradicting that expectation |
| `B_interface` | **Contact region** | edits on or beside residues that physically touch HER2, excluding CDR-H3 | second best; produced two of the significant designs |
| `C_mixed` | **Mixed** | a few edits near the contact surface plus supporting edits elsewhere | produced the most statistically significant designs |
| `D_framework` | **Scaffold only** | edits only in the structural framework; the binding site is left untouched | best average interface quality, and the safest edits |

**The headline surprise:** CDR-H3 editing is the literature-favoured approach and it
performed worst here, while leaving the binding site alone (scaffold-only) preserved
the interface best. Our reading is that trastuzumab's binding loops are already
affinity-matured, so changing them is far more likely to break the grip than improve
it. All four strategies are still represented in the nominations, because one
campaign on one target is not enough to retire a hypothesis.
